# Climate-Based Poverty Profile of Tanzania

## Overview

This repository contains the final research project completed for the course **Poverty and Inequality (ECO392)** at **Shiv Nadar Institution of Eminence**.

The study examines how rainfall patterns and climate stability influence household poverty and welfare outcomes across Tanzania. Using nationally representative household survey data and subnational rainfall data, the project investigates whether regions with higher agricultural productivity necessarily experience lower poverty and identifies the role of climate stability, infrastructure, and market access in shaping welfare outcomes.

## Research Question

How do rainfall patterns and climate stability influence household poverty and welfare outcomes across Tanzania?

## Dataset

The analysis combines:

* Tanzania Household Budget Survey (2017–18)
* Subnational rainfall data
* Rainy-season crop yield statistics

The final dataset covers **8,757 households** across Tanzania.

Households were classified into four climate regimes based on rainfall quantity and rainfall stability:

* Wet + Stable
* Wet + Unstable
* Dry + Stable
* Dry + Unstable

## Methodology

Household welfare was measured using the OECD-modified adult-equivalence consumption scale, allowing fair comparisons across households of different sizes and compositions.

Poverty was analysed using the Foster-Greer-Thorbecke (FGT) poverty indices to estimate:

* Poverty Headcount Ratio
* Poverty Gap
* Poverty Severity

The results were then compared across the four climate regimes and cross-referenced with regional crop yield data to examine whether higher agricultural productivity translated into lower poverty.

## Key Findings

* Wet + Unstable regions recorded the highest levels of poverty despite receiving relatively high rainfall.
* Dry + Stable regions showed the strongest welfare outcomes despite comparatively lower agricultural yields.
* Higher rainfall and higher crop yields did not necessarily correspond to lower poverty.
* Climate stability, infrastructure, and market access appeared to play a more important role in improving welfare than rainfall volume alone.
* The findings highlight the importance of income diversification and institutional support in reducing vulnerability.

## Policy Recommendations

The project recommends:

* Expanding irrigation and climate-resilient farming practices.
* Strengthening transport infrastructure and market connectivity.
* Prioritising climate adaptation planning in vulnerable regions.
* Expanding shock-responsive social protection programmes such as TASAF.
* Promoting income diversification to reduce dependence on rainfall-sensitive agriculture.
